Build a single-file browser Rubik's cube game, rubiks-cube.html, from the spec below. It's a 3D cube I already had (I don't have the original file, so rebuild it from the Baseline section) plus three new features: CFOP hints, an algorithms tab for learning beginner CFOP, and a csTimer-style speedcubing timer.

## Baseline (rebuild exactly)
Full-window 3D cube on a dark background, keyboard controlled, with Scramble/Solve/Reset buttons at the top left.
- 27 cubies: near-black bodies with colored stickers inset on the outward faces. Colors: U white, D yellow, F green, B blue, R red, L orange.
- Perspective camera orbits the cube. Dragging (mouse or touch) rotates the view, and the vertical angle is clamped so it never flips. Ambient light plus a directional light that follows the camera. Resizes with the window. *(Added later:)* the mouse wheel and a two-finger pinch zoom the view.
- Keys: U D L R F B turn that face 90° clockwise (as seen looking at that face). X Y Z rotate the whole cube (X follows R, Y follows U, Z follows F). Shift + key reverses the turn. Held-key auto-repeat is ignored.
- Turns animate over about 160 ms with ease in-out. Keypresses queue and play in order. After a whole-cube rotation, face keys refer to whatever face is now in that position. Snap positions and rotations to exact values after each turn so drift never builds up.
- Scramble: 25 random quarter turns, never the same face twice in a row, animated fast.
- Solve: *(changed later)* plays a short CFOP solution from the hint solver (`Solver.solve`), so it works from any state, including a scanned cube. If the cube changes before the solution arrives, it is dropped. *(Original baseline: play back the inverse of every move since the cube was last solved, with a move and its inverse cancelling out of the history. The history is still tracked by `Baseline.createHistory`.)*
- Reset: instantly restores a solved cube and clears the queue and history.
- A green "Solved" label shows whenever the cube is solved and idle, in any orientation.
- Bottom help line lists the keys and "drag to rotate the view".

## Feature 1: CFOP hints
- A Hints button in the top bar shows its on/off state. The H key also toggles it.
- The hint panel shows: the current stage, the goal of the next step in plain words, the case name if there is one, and the move sequence with the next move marked. The layer for the next move is highlighted on the 3D cube.
- If I play the suggested move, the panel advances. If I play any other move, the hint recomputes.
- Hints are hidden while a timed solve is running.
- Stages are checked in this order (the first one not done is the current stage):
  1. Orientation: white center must be on D. Hint the rotation that puts it there.
  2. Cross: 4 white edges on D, each matching its side center. Hint a move sequence, ideally optimal (8 moves max).
  3. F2L: all 4 corner-edge pairs inserted. Hint the easiest next pair, with moves that don't break the cross or pairs already solved.
  4. OLL edges: yellow cross on U. Hint the case (dot, L, line), any U setup turn, and the alg.
  5. OLL corners: whole U face yellow. Hint the case, setup turn, and alg.
  6. PLL corners permuted: hint the case (adjacent or diagonal swap), setup turn, and alg.
  7. PLL edges, solved up to a U turn: hint Ua/Ub/H/Z, setup turn, and alg.
  8. AUF: the final U turn.
- The app must track sticker colors, not just 3D transforms. The move system must support R2, wide moves (r, f), M, and D, both for logic and for animation.
- OLL/PLL hints must use exactly the algorithms in Feature 2.
- Computing a hint must never freeze the page (use a Web Worker or keep it fast).

## Feature 2: Algorithms tab
Add a top bar tab switch: Cube | Algorithms. The Algorithms tab has one card per case. Each card shows:
- The case name and group.
- A case diagram generated by applying the inverse of the alg to a solved cube, so a diagram can never mismatch its alg:
  - OLL: top-down view of U plus the side stickers of the top layer, yellow vs gray only.
  - PLL: the same view in full color, with arrows for the pieces that swap.
  - F2L: a 3D-looking view of U/F/R with only the cross, the target pair and the centers colored; everything else gray.
- The alg in standard notation.
- A mini animated cube that starts in the case state and plays the alg, with Play/Pause, step back, step forward and Reset controls, and the current move highlighted in the alg text.

Algorithms (20):
- F2L basic inserts (slot front-right, cross on D):
  - Corner UFR white facing front, edge UR: U R U' R'
  - Corner UFR white facing right, edge UF: U' F' U F
  - Corner UFR white facing right, edge UB: R U R'
  - Corner UFR white facing front, edge UL: F' U' F
- OLL edges: Line (hold horizontal) F R U R' U' F' | L (hold at back-left) f R U R' U' f' | Dot F R U R' U' F' f R U R' U' f'
- OLL corners: Sune R U R' U R U2 R' | Antisune R U2 R' U' R U' R' | H R U R' U R U' R' U R U2 R' | Pi R U2 R2 U' R2 U' R2 U2 R | Headlights R2 D R' U2 R D' R' U2 R' | Chameleon r U R' U' r' F R F' | Bowtie F' r U R' U' r' F R
- PLL corners: Adjacent swap (T) R U R' U' R' F R2 U' R' U' R U R' F' | Diagonal swap (Y) F R U' R' U' R U R' F' R U R' U' R' F R F'
- PLL edges: Ua R U' R U R U R U' R' U' R2 | Ub R2 U R U R' U' R' U' R' U R' | H M2 U M2 U2 M2 U M2 | Z M' U M2 U M2 U M' U2 M2
Verify every alg in code: starting from its own case state, it must leave its target (the slot, the U face, or the whole cube) solved, allowing one final U turn. If an alg fails, fix it and tell me which one.

## Feature 3: Timer (Cube tab, behaves like csTimer)
1. Show a 20-move scramble as text and apply it to the cube, either when I trigger a timer scramble or after the previous solve finishes.
2. Optional 15 s WCA inspection, off by default: space starts the countdown. Over 15 s = +2, over 17 s = DNF.
3. Hold space: the timer goes red, then green after 0.3 s. Releasing before green cancels.
4. Releasing on green starts the timer: large digits, 0.01 s precision.
5. The timer stops when the cube is solved (any orientation) or when I press space. Escape stops it as a DNF.
- Session list, newest first: each time with +2, DNF and delete actions, plus its scramble. Show current, best, ao5, ao12 and mean. Averages use WCA rules: drop best and worst; more than one DNF in an ao5 makes it a DNF.
- Save times in localStorage, wrapped in try/catch.
- Face and rotation keys keep working while timing. Space is reserved for the timer. During a timed solve, hints are hidden and Solve/Scramble/Reset are disabled. Releasing space must never trigger a focused button.

## Feature 4: Scan a real cube (added after the first build)
A third top bar tab, Cube | Algorithms | Scan, loads a physical cube's state onto the 3D cube so hints and Solve work on it.
- **Net editor.** An unfolded net (U above, L F R B in a row, D below) of 54 stickers plus a 6-colour palette. Click or drag to paint stickers. Unset stickers are gray. Painting a centre a different colour swaps those two colours everywhere on the net, so a whole colour group can be corrected in one click. Buttons: Clear (centres only), From cube (copy the current cube), Load onto cube.
- **Validation**, shown live in plain words, with the offending stickers outlined: unset stickers, colour counts other than 9, centres that no real cube has (white opposite yellow, green opposite blue, red opposite orange, standard chirality), pieces that don't exist or appear twice, a twisted corner, a flipped edge, and swapped-piece parity. Load onto cube stays disabled until the net is valid.
- **Webcam scan.** Start camera opens the webcam (getUserMedia), with a 3×3 guide over the video whose cells are tinted live with the colour each one reads. Six guided captures, holding the cube as told:
  1. Front: any face to the camera; keep the same side on top for steps 1–4.
  2–4. Right, Back, Left: each time, turn the whole cube a quarter turn to the left.
  5. Up: one more quarter turn left (front faces you again), then tip the top toward the camera.
  6. Down: tip the cube over half a turn so the bottom faces the camera.
  Each step chip can be clicked to redo that face. After the sixth capture the colours are classified and the net is filled in for review. The camera stops when leaving the tab.
- **Colour classification.** Each sticker is read as the median colour of the middle of its cell. The six centres decide which colour is which: of the 24 orientations of a standard cube, pick the one whose colours best match the centre readings (chirality settles red vs orange). Every sticker is then assigned to the centre it is closest to in Lab colour space, with exactly 9 stickers per colour.
- **Loading.** Load onto cube puts the state on the 3D cube in the orientation it was scanned (Front faces you), clears the move history, refreshes hints, and switches to the Cube tab. The Scan tab is disabled during a timed solve, like Algorithms.
- No external libraries; the page stays a single file.

Acceptance for Feature 4:
- The validator accepts every state reached by random moves (including M, wide moves and rotations) and rejects each kind of error above with the right message.
- Every validated scrambled state is solved by the Solve button's solver.
- The capture steps map camera cells to the correct facelets (checked against the whole-cube rotations the instructions describe).
- Classification recovers the exact state from synthetic camera readings with noise, a brightness change and a colour-temperature shift, for random cube orientations.
- In a browser with a fake camera stream, six captures fill the net and Load puts that state on the cube.

## Feature 5: Low-effort scanning (added after Feature 4)
Scanning should need as few clicks and as little care as possible. Builds on Feature 4's webcam scan.
- **Auto-capture.** On by default (an "Auto-capture" checkbox turns it off). A face is captured automatically once it has been held steady in the guide for about 0.75 s. It counts as a face when every cell is one even colour and either the cells show at least two colours or dark gaps separate the cells (so a solved face counts, a blank wall doesn't). After a capture, the cube must move before the next one, and a face that matches one already captured is never captured again. That way, holding a face still never captures it twice. The guide border shows the state: white while waiting, yellow while holding steady, a green flash on capture. The Capture button stays for manual use (for example, a stickerless cube with a solved face).
- **Auto-advance.** Each capture moves on to the next unscanned step, and the sixth fills the net. Clicking a step chip rescans that face.
- **Guided by centre colour.** *(Supersedes Feature 4's Front/Right/Back/Left/Up/Down steps.)* Each step names a centre colour and the colour to keep on top: green, red, blue and orange with white on top (a quarter turn left each time), then white with blue on top, then yellow with green on top. The step chips show each centre's colour, and the instruction line shows the centre colour the camera sees right now. A capture goes to the step of the centre colour it shows, choosing the closest colour among the unscanned steps and the selected step, so the faces can be shown in any order. When every step is filled, a capture replaces the closest colour.
- **Any rotation, mistakes tolerated.** When the six captures don't make a valid cube as held, each capture's face is worked out from its centre colour and its rotation from the shared edges and corners. The combination that makes the most real pieces wins, a valid cube always wins, and ties go to the holds as instructed. A note says when faces were put right.
- **Loading orientation.** *(Supersedes Feature 4's "in the orientation it was scanned".)* The scanned cube loads in the standard orientation: white on top, green in front.
- **Steady camera.** After the first capture, the camera's exposure and white balance are locked at their current values where the camera allows it (best effort; the readings already tolerate exposure changes). They unlock when the camera stops.
- **Mid-solve cubes.** Any valid state can be scanned, including a partly solved cube with whole faces of one colour.

Acceptance for Feature 5:
- Captures given in any order and any rotation, with camera noise, give the same state as captures held as instructed, with white on top and green in front.
- A capture goes to the step of its centre colour among the steps it may fill.
- The auto-capture logic (in `scan.js`, no DOM) captures a steady face exactly once, waits for movement, ignores a repeated face, captures a uniform face only when it has gaps, and ignores a blank or moving view.
- In a browser with a fake camera that shows the six faces in a shuffled order and turned, without pressing Capture, every face lands on its colour's step and the net fills in and validates.

## Feature 6: Race (added after Feature 5)
After scrambling, or loading a scanned cube, race the digital cube with a real one: the digital cube solves itself like a speedcuber while I solve my physical cube.
- **Race mode of the timer.** A "Race" checkbox in the timer panel on the Cube tab (off by default). When it is on, a row below it sets the ghost's target: a time in seconds, or "Use my PB". Hold space and release on green as in Feature 3, with optional inspection.
- **The ghost.** On release, the digital cube solves itself from its current state with the `Solver.solve` solution, while my timer runs.
- **Pacing.** A human-like CFOP rhythm, not a constant rate: a recognition pause before each stage (cross, each F2L pair, OLL, PLL, AUF); slower turning in the cross and F2L, fast bursts inside last-layer algorithms; small random variation from move to move; double turns and cube rotations take longer than quarter turns. Everything is scaled so the ghost finishes within a few percent of the target.
- **Target time.** A set time in seconds (default 20), remembered in localStorage with the Race and PB choices (wrapped in try/catch). "Use my PB" uses the best non-DNF single in the session, including +2. With no PB yet, the set time is used and the panel says so. Turns can't animate faster than about 60 ms (90 ms for double turns and rotations); if the target is faster than that allows for this solution, the panel says so and the ghost takes the fastest time it can.
- **During a race.** Face and rotation keys don't move the digital cube. Scramble, Solve, Reset, Hints and the other tabs are disabled, as during a timed solve, until both the ghost and I have finished. The digital cube reaching solved doesn't stop my timer: I stop it with space, and Escape stops it as a DNF, as in Feature 3.
- **Result.** When the ghost finishes, the panel shows its time while my timer keeps running. When I stop, it shows both times, who won and by how much (a DNF loses; if the ghost is still solving, I won and its time appears when it finishes). My time is saved to the session as a normal solve, marked "race" in the list. The ghost's time is not saved and isn't in the session stats.
- If the cube is already solved when I try to start a race, it doesn't start, and the panel says to scramble first.

Acceptance for Feature 6:
- The pacing schedule (pure, in `race.js`) finishes within 2% of the target for 200 seeded scrambles at targets from 8 s to 60 s, and reports a cap when the target is below the animation limit.
- Every stage starts with a pause, last-layer turns are faster than F2L turns, and double turns take longer than quarter turns in the same stage.
- The PB lookup skips DNFs, counts +2, and returns none for an empty or all-DNF session; the target falls back to the set time with a note.
- In race mode, the cube becoming solved doesn't stop the timer; space does, and the entry is saved with its race mark and survives a reload; stats are unchanged by the ghost.
- In a browser: scramble, Race with an 8 s target, release space: the digital cube solves in about 8 s, my timer keeps running after it, and pressing space shows both times and the winner.

## Feature 7: Notation (added later, inside the Algorithms tab)
"Add a tab in the Algorithms tab that goes over all notation, with clear visualization of each, like F, F', f, etc."
- **Notation** is a choice next to All / F2L / OLL / PLL in the Algorithms tab filter. Choosing it shows notation cards instead of alg cards (and is remembered like the other filters).
- A short intro in plain words: a letter alone is a clockwise quarter turn of that face, as seen looking straight at that face; `'` (prime) is counterclockwise; `2` is a half turn; lowercase, or `Rw`, turns two layers; slices turn the middle layer; x, y and z turn the whole cube.
- One card per move family, in four groups:
  - Face turns: U, D, L, R, F, B.
  - Wide turns: u, d, l, r, f, b (each card says `Rw` means the same as `r`).
  - Slices: M (follows L), E (follows D), S (follows F).
  - Rotations: x (follows R), y (follows U), z (follows F).
- Each card shows the name, a one-line description (for slices and rotations, which face it follows), buttons for its variants (X, X', X2), and a mini 3D cube that plays the selected variant in a loop from a solved cube (white top, green front).
  - The cubies that don't move are dimmed, so the moving layer(s) stand out.
  - A pink curved arrow around the turning layer shows the direction (two arrowheads for a half turn).
  - The camera starts where the turning face is easy to see. Cards for moves that turn a face hidden from the default view say so ("Seen from behind", "Seen from below", "Seen from the left"). The mini cube can still be dragged.
- **E and S** are added to `core.js`: E is the layer between U and D, turning like D; S is the layer between F and B, turning like F. Both take `'` and `2`, and the main cube can play them.
- No new top-bar buttons.

Acceptance for Feature 7:
- E and S invert, E = U D' y', S = F' B z, and every existing test still passes (solver, animation mapping, scan validator).
- For every notation card and variant, the mini cube's end state equals `Cube.applyMove(solved, move)`, its dimmed cubies are exactly the ones that don't move, and its arrow turns the same way as the move.
- In a browser, the Notation cards show the highlighted layer and the arrow mid-animation (checked for B, M and x).

## Feature 8: Custom keybinds (added later)
"The user should be able to change keybinds to whatever they would like for digital solves."
- **Actions:** every face turn U D L R F B, wide turn u d l r f b, slice M E S and rotation x y z, each as clockwise, prime and 2, plus "Toggle hints".
- **Defaults** reproduce the Baseline keys exactly: the letter turns clockwise and Shift+letter is prime, for U D L R F B, M and X Y Z; Alt+face (with or without Shift) is the wide turn; H (with or without Shift) toggles hints. Everything else starts unbound.
- **Binding:** any key, optionally with Shift and/or Alt. Several keys per action are allowed (for csTimer-style two-handed layouts). Letters are the letter printed on the key, as before; other keys are the physical key. Space and Escape are reserved for the timer and can't be bound. Binding a key that another action uses moves it to the new action and says so.
- **Keys panel:** opened from a "Keys" control at the end of the bottom help line on the Cube tab (not a top-bar button; disabled while timing). It lists every action with its keys, each key with a remove button, an "Add key" button that waits for the next key press ("press a key to bind", Escape cancels), "Reset to defaults" and "Close". While the panel is open, key presses never move the cube or reach the timer, and Escape closes it.
- The bottom help line is generated from the current bindings.
- Bindings are saved in localStorage (wrapped in try/catch, like the timer). Missing or corrupt storage falls back to the defaults.
- Baseline rules still hold: auto-repeat is ignored, moves queue as before, and Space never presses a button.

Acceptance for Feature 8:
- The default keymap gives the same move as the old key mapping for every letter with every Shift/Alt combination, and H toggles hints.
- Rebinding, moving a key from another action, Space/Escape refused, several keys per action, and Shift/Alt handling are tested in Node.
- Bindings survive serialise/parse; corrupt or invalid storage gives the defaults.
- In a browser: rebinding U to J and U' to F makes J turn U and F turn U'; the bindings survive a reload; Space still runs the timer.
